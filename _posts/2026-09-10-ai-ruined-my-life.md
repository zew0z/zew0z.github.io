# AI Ruined My Life

If I still had my X account, I would post exactly this: **"AI ruined my life. Stop it before you lose yours."**

That's not a joke title. I said it out loud this week, mid-crisis, and I meant it. This post is the full story of how I went from "I want to learn cybersecurity" to spending entire days building AI setups, and how I got out.

---

## How It Started (It Made Sense, I Swear)

The logic felt airtight. I kept hitting friction doing anything: studying, logging progress, staying organized. So naturally: build a system. Then the system needs an agent. Then the agent needs to be voice-controlled, so it can manage my computer and open apps for me. Then that needs an agent site. Then that needs dictation. Then I'm making custom bots at 1 a.m.

I thought all of these tools were going to help me. Every single one felt like progress. "Once this is set up, I'll finally be locked in."

## Three Clicks

Here's the sentence that broke my brain, which I also said out loud:

> Opening TryHackMe takes just three clicks, but I keep being like, "Oh, how can I make an AI agent that's better?"

Three. Clicks. The actual work I'm avoiding takes three clicks, and I'm drafting architecture for an always-on voice agent to save myself those three clicks. I didn't need any of this a year ago. The work was never the problem. The work was always sitting right there, three clicks away, while I was upstream compiling tooling for work I hadn't started.

## The Mac Incident

A shiny new model drops. Computer use, "complete intelligence", the whole announcement-hype package. It doesn't run on Linux, my actual daily driver that I love because it's productive.

So for about an hour I genuinely planned to go back to my MacBook. The laggy one. The one I hate.

And you know what? I bought the subscription anyway, for a model I can't even use on my main machine. The usage sucks. It saves me basically zero time. I did it because a new model existed and my brain treated that as a problem I needed to solve with money.

## The Five-Hour Trap

One recent day, honestly reconstructed from my own notes: gym in the morning, back by 6:30 p.m., and then five straight hours of installing things, building an AI agent site that failed miserably, and creating more bots. Total study time that day: about 20 minutes, with dozing off included.

The worst part is how busy I felt. I was executing constantly. But "executing" was installing and configuring and tweaking, never the actual thing. I realize now I was even afraid to focus on real work, because what if the agent finished installing and interrupted me?

## Autopilot Brain

When I *was* using the tools, my mind was on autopilot anyway. I'd send a really vague prompt, not even a real one. "Audit this." Then I'd sit there doing random stuff while it "worked", consuming AI content while waiting for AI output. My YouTube feed had become a self-improvement-AI-building rabbit hole and my feed was nothing but AI news, builds, concepts, improvements. Same content, different day. I kept hearing the same advice over and over and calling it learning.

## The Realization

I had to say the obvious out loud before it clicked:

> It's not like I'm building big projects. It's not like I'm selling to clients. I'm just trying to learn cybersecurity through bug bounties.

That's the whole mission statement. And for that mission, my existing stack was already 100% complete. The friction I was trying to tool my way out of wasn't in my workflow. It was the tooling.

## What I Actually Did

- Blocked the AI news feeds. Cleaned my entire YouTube feed of the "build with AI" rabbit hole.
- Kept **one** AI plan: a fast model for talking my thoughts out loud and logging my notes. That's it. No agents managing my computer, no bot fleet, no announcements-chasing.
- Redefined the stack to the boring minimum: Linux, voice notes, TryHackMe. Done. Nothing gets added unless a real project demands it.
- Started scheduling deliberate boredom, because the honest diagnosis was: "I just need to be bored enough and actually go do the stuff."

And it worked. The next day I woke up early, rooted the Mr Robot box, and wrote the writeup you can read right above this post. First try, no new tools required.

## What I Learned

- **The tool is not the work.** Meta-work is procrastination wearing a productivity costume.
- **Every new model announcement feels like progress. It's consumption.** You're not falling behind by skipping a launch.
- **"Fast enough" beats "best".** A slow flagship model loses you to distraction while it types.
- **If your setup needs you to keep building the setup, you already lost.**
- **Boredom is not the enemy. It's the signal that puts you back on the actual work.**

The funny ending: this post exists because I voice-logged my entire crisis to an AI assistant, and that conversation is what talked me out of buying more AI. The one subscription I kept is the one that just listens and takes notes. That's the only feature I actually needed.

**Stop it before you lose yours.**
